How I prompt

Great output starts with a great brief.

AI is only as precise as the instructions it gets. This is the structure behind every project on this page, shown on the brief for the Frost Titan.

GOALDesign the new final-tier monster for Monster Merge: the Frost Titan, Tier 11, Secret.

CONTEXTIt merges from two Cosmic Titans. Players mostly see it small, from the board camera, on an 8-stud cell.

CONSTRAINTSFootprint under 7.8 studs, height under 10. SmoothPlastic like every monster, Neon only for eyes and heart. Mutations never recolour the eyes.

REFERENCEIt has to read as part of the same low-poly family as the other ten species.

OUTPUTTurnaround views, palette, a rig with joints and a seamless idle loop. Then build it in Studio.

PROOFShow it still reads at 72 px, stands on its tile in all four looks and matches the blockout part for part.

GoalOne sentence that says exactly what you want. A vague goal gets you a generic result.
    Numbers over adjectives“Under 10 studs” beats “not too big”. Measurable beats vague.
    Phases, not one shotDesign, then core loop, then polish. I review every phase before the next.
    Make it prove itTests, logs, screenshots, 200,000 test rolls. Claims need evidence.
    Ask what wasn't testedEvery report lists what could not be checked yet, so the blind spots are known.
    Humans decideStyle, scope and what ships stay my call. AI does the heavy lifting.
